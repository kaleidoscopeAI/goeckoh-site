F0 — Fundamental Frequency (Pitch)
— Hz
The rate at which your vocal folds open and snap shut. Every second, an adult male's folds close ~116 times; an adult female's ~205 times; a child's ~223 times. That repetition rate is what you hear as pitch.

Goeckoh measures F0 by autocorrelation in the 80–500 Hz range and preserves it exactly — the correction never moves pitch. Your speaker identity lives in F0.
Normal adult male: 93–135 Hz · Female: 162–238 Hz · Child: ~223 Hz
📍
F1 — First Formant (Tongue Height)
— Hz
The lowest resonance peak of your vocal tract. F1 is controlled by jaw openness and tongue height. Open your jaw wide — F1 rises. Raise your tongue toward the palate — F1 falls.

In ASD and dysarthria, F1 often fails to reach its target because jaw excursion is reduced (articulatory undershoot). Goeckoh shifts F1's resonance pole toward the clinical target using a DF2T biquad filter.
Typical range across vowels: 300–900 Hz
↔️
F2 — Second Formant (Tongue Front/Back)
— Hz
The second resonance peak. F2 encodes tongue advancement. Push your tongue forward — F2 rises (front vowels like /i/). Pull it back — F2 falls (back vowels like /u/).

In dysarthria, back vowels are the hardest to produce correctly — both lip-rounding and tongue retraction are reduced simultaneously, pushing F2 into front-vowel territory. This is measurable and correctable.
Typical range across vowels: 800–2500 Hz
〰️
HNR — Harmonics-to-Noise Ratio
— dB
The ratio of periodic (harmonic) energy to turbulent noise in your glottal signal. A high HNR means your vocal folds are vibrating cleanly and regularly. A low HNR means airflow is leaking turbulently — a breathy, rough, or pathological voice quality.

HNR is computed from the autocorrelation peak at the F0 lag period. Goeckoh logs HNR continuously during enrollment to characterize your baseline voice quality.
Healthy adult on sustained vowel: ≥11 dB (minimum: ≥7.4 dB)
🌊
What is a Formant?
Vocal tract resonance
Your throat, mouth, and nasal cavity form a ~17 cm resonant tube. Like any tube, it has natural frequencies where sound energy is amplified — these are the formants.

When vocal fold buzz travels up through the tract, certain frequencies get boosted (at formants) and others get suppressed. The resulting spectral envelope — the shape drawn over the harmonic peaks — defines the vowel. Different mouth shapes = different formant positions = different vowels.

This is Fant's source-filter theory (1960): the folds produce the source; the tract is the filter. They are independent. Goeckoh corrects only the filter — never the source.
F1 encodes height · F2 encodes front/back · F3 encodes rhotics + identity

Why Hearing Yourself Matters
Corollary discharge
Every time your motor cortex sends a speech command, it simultaneously sends a copy — an efference copy — to your auditory cortex. The auditory cortex uses this copy to predict what you should sound like.

If the heard sound matches the prediction, the brain confirms success and suppresses further correction. If it doesn't match, a prediction error signal drives learning.

In ASD and dysarthria, the child produces a distorted vowel, the auditory cortex predicts a distorted vowel, the sounds match — and the broken pattern is reinforced. Goeckoh breaks this cycle by delivering a corrected signal inside the ~200 ms window before the prediction comparison closes. The brain hears itself succeed.
Prediction window: ~200ms · Goeckoh delivery: <50ms · Margin: 150ms+
