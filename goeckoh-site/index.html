The Problem

Speech disorders compress the acoustic vowel space.

A vowel is defined by two numbers: F1 (first formant, ~300–900 Hz — encodes tongue height / jaw openness) and F2 (second formant, ~800–2500 Hz — encodes tongue front/back position).2

In ASD, dysarthria, and apraxia, articulatory undershoot means vowels fail to reach their canonical positions in the F1/F2 plane. The entire vowel quadrilateral compresses toward the center — toward the neutral schwa /ə/. This is measurable as a reduced Vowel Space Area (VSA) and elevated Formant Centralization Ratio (FCR).3

In TORGO corpus speakers with dysarthria, intended back vowels like /u/ showed F2 values of 1563–1935 Hz — typical front-vowel territory — because lip-rounding and tongue retraction were both reduced simultaneously.4 The formant cue is not just noisy — it can be physically absent.

Goeckoh measures this displacement in Bark scale (the perceptual frequency scale the cochlea actually uses5), and corrects it in real time.

Neuroscience

The 200 ms window.
Every therapeutic intervention
must fit inside it.

When your motor cortex issues a speech command, it simultaneously sends an efference copy to the auditory cortex — a prediction of what the sound should be.1 The auditory cortex holds this prediction open for approximately 200 ms. If the heard sound matches the prediction, suppression occurs (the brain says "expected"). If it doesn't, a prediction error drives corrective learning. Goeckoh delivers corrected audio in <50 ms — well inside this window — so the brain accepts the corrected formants as its own successful production.

⚡
Efference Copy (PNAS 2024)
A corollary discharge signal travels from ventral motor cortex to auditory cortex before speech onset, predicting the sound the command will produce. This has been confirmed in direct human electrophysiology and predicts the degree of auditory suppression.1
🔇
Speaking-Induced Suppression
Self-generated speech produces a smaller auditory cortex response than identical externally-played sound — the predicted N100/N1 suppression. The amount of suppression scales with how well the heard sound matches the prediction. A mismatch produces a learning signal.6
🧠
DIVA Model (Guenther)
Speech production uses feedforward motor commands plus continuous auditory and somatosensory feedback control. During development, hearing one's own vocalizations tunes the sensorimotor maps. Disrupted auditory-motor interaction → motor speech disorders. Goeckoh re-closes that loop.7
📐
Source–Filter Independence
Vocal folds (source: F0, pitch, voice quality) and vocal tract (filter: formants) are acoustically independent — Fant, 1960.2 Goeckoh exploits this: LPC separates source from filter, shifts formant poles toward clinical targets, re-synthesizes using the original excitation. F0 is preserved to the sample — the voice identity is intrinsically maintained.

Formant correction filter — DF2T biquad
H(z) = (1 − 2r·cos(ωsrc)·z−1 + r²·z−2) / (1 − 2r·cos(ωtgt)·z−1 + r²·z−2)

The zero-pair (numerator) cancels the current formant resonance at ωsrc. The pole-pair (denominator) adds the target resonance at ωtgt. Bandwidth r = exp(−π × BW / sr) is shared — formant bandwidth is preserved, not stretched. F1 and F2 corrections cascade as two independent biquad sections operating at the full 48 kHz sample rate.
